Japji Sahib — Mool Mantar, Opening Salok & Pauri 1

ੴ Mool Mantar

ੴ — Ik Oankaar

One ultimate Reality is.

Everything that exists has its ground in One. God is not merely one being among other beings; the Divine is the underlying source and reality of existence itself.

⸻

ਸਤਿ ਨਾਮੁ — Sat Naam

Its nature is Truth / Eternal Reality.

Sat is more than simply “telling the truth.” It points to that which truly is and does not cease to be.

Everything changes, but the Reality underlying everything remains.

⸻

ਕਰਤਾ ਪੁਰਖੁ — Kartaa Purakh

The Creative Being; the One who acts and creates.

The One is not distant from creation. The creative Divine presence operates throughout existence.

⸻

ਨਿਰਭਉ — Nirbhau

Without fear.

The Infinite has nothing outside itself that could threaten it.

⸻

ਨਿਰਵੈਰੁ — Nirvair

Without hatred or enmity.

The Divine has no enemies, resentment, vengeance, or hostility.

⸻

ਅਕਾਲ ਮੂਰਤਿ — Akaal Moorat

Timeless Being.

Everything we experience exists within time and therefore changes.

The Divine Reality is not subject to aging, decay, or death.

⸻

ਅਜੂਨੀ — Ajooni

Unborn; beyond the cycle of birth.

Forms are born and die. The Reality underlying them is not itself born.

⸻

ਸੈਭੰ — Saibhang

Self-existent.

Its existence depends upon nothing else. Nothing created or caused the One.

⸻

ਗੁਰ ਪ੍ਰਸਾਦਿ — Gur Prasaad

Realized through the Guru’s grace.

This Reality is not ultimately reached through intellectual argument alone. It becomes known through the Guru’s illumination and grace.

⸻

ਜਪੁ — Jap

Contemplate. Remember. Meditate upon this.

The Mool Mantar has described the Reality. Jap invites us to turn our awareness toward it.

⸻

Opening Salok

ਆਦਿ ਸਚੁ — Aad Sach

True before the beginning.

ਜੁਗਾਦਿ ਸਚੁ — Jugaad Sach

True throughout the ages.

ਹੈ ਭੀ ਸਚੁ — Hai Bhee Sach

True even now.

ਨਾਨਕ ਹੋਸੀ ਭੀ ਸਚੁ — Nanak Hosee Bhee Sach

Nanak says: Truth shall also remain.

The essential meaning

Before everything — It is.
Throughout existence — It is.
Now — It is.
Forever — It will be.

This establishes what Guru Nanak means by Sat: Reality is not something that comes into existence and later disappears.

⸻

Pauri 1

Having described what Reality is, Guru Nanak now turns to the human problem:

How do we actually realize and live in accordance with this Reality?

⸻

1. ਸੋਚੈ ਸੋਚਿ ਨ ਹੋਵਈ ਜੇ ਸੋਚੀ ਲਖ ਵਾਰ ॥

Sochey soch na hovai, je sochey lakh vaar.

Meaning:
Truth cannot be attained merely by multiplying our own mental or ritual efforts, even endlessly.

Soch can carry the sense of thinking/contemplation as well as purification or ritual purity.

The broader message is that Reality cannot be reached simply by trying harder and harder through our own mental or ritual effort.

⸻

2. ਚੁਪੈ ਚੁਪ ਨ ਹੋਵਈ ਜੇ ਲਾਇ ਰਹਾ ਲਿਵ ਤਾਰ ॥

Chupe chup na hovai, je lai raha liv taar.

Meaning:
Inner silence is not attained merely by remaining outwardly silent, even through prolonged concentration.

A person can stop speaking, withdraw from others, and sit completely still while the mind continues producing:

* thoughts,
* desires,
* memories,
* fears,
* worries.

Therefore:

Physical silence is not necessarily inner stillness.

⸻

3. ਭੁਖਿਆ ਭੁਖ ਨ ਉਤਰੀ ਜੇ ਬੰਨਾ ਪੁਰੀਆ ਭਾਰ ॥

Bhukhya bhukh na utri, je banna puriya bhaar.

Meaning:
The hunger of desire is not satisfied even if one accumulates the wealth of entire worlds.

Bhukh here can be understood as more than physical hunger. It is the continuing human feeling:

“I need something more.”

Money, possessions, recognition, status, pleasure, power — even spiritual achievements — can become objects of this hunger.

The insight is profound:

Satisfying one desire does not necessarily end desire. Another often takes its place.

⸻

4. ਸਹਸ ਸਿਆਣਪਾ ਲਖ ਹੋਹਿ ਤ ਇਕ ਨ ਚਲੈ ਨਾਲਿ ॥

Sehas sayaanpa lakh hoeh, ta ek na chalai naal.

Meaning:
One may possess countless clever ideas and intellectual accomplishments, yet not even one ultimately accompanies us.

Human intelligence and cleverness have limits.

We can accumulate enormous knowledge and still fail to understand the fundamental nature of existence.

At death, our intellectual accomplishments cannot accompany us.

Guru Nanak has now removed four possible solutions

Human approach	Why it is insufficient
Mental/ritual effort	Cannot by itself produce realization
Outward silence	Does not guarantee inner stillness
Fulfilling desires	Does not end desire
Intellectual cleverness	Cannot penetrate ultimate Reality by itself

This brings us to the central question.

⸻

The Central Question

ਕਿਵ ਸਚਿਆਰਾ ਹੋਈਐ ਕਿਵ ਕੂੜੈ ਤੁਟੈ ਪਾਲਿ ॥

Kiv sachiaara hoiye? Kiv koorai tuttai paal?

How does one become Sachiaar — one who lives in Truth?
How is the wall of falsehood broken?

This is one of the central questions of Japji Sahib.

Guru Nanak is not merely asking:

“How can I know that God exists?”

The question is much deeper:

How can I become aligned with Reality itself?

Koor means falsehood, but in this context it can be understood more deeply as the false, ego-centered perception through which we experience ourselves as separate and independent from the greater Reality.

How does that wall fall?

Guru Nanak now gives the answer.

⸻

The Answer — Hukam

ਹੁਕਮਿ ਰਜਾਈ ਚਲਣਾ ਨਾਨਕ ਲਿਖਿਆ ਨਾਲਿ ॥੧॥

Hukam rajai chalna, Nanak likhya naal.

Meaning:

Nanak says: live in accordance with Hukam — the Divine Order woven into existence.

Hukam is often translated simply as “God’s command.”

But its meaning here is richer:

Divine Order
The way Reality operates
The principle through which existence unfolds

Guru Nanak’s answer is therefore not:

Escape from the world.

Rather:

Stop demanding that Reality conform to the ego, and learn to live in harmony with Reality as it actually is.

⸻

The Flow of Pauri 1

The whole Pauri can be understood as a progression:

Can endless purification/thinking reveal Truth?
↓
No.

Can silence and intense meditation alone do it?
↓
No.

Can satisfying every desire do it?
↓
No.

Can enormous intelligence and cleverness do it?
↓
No.

Then how do I become Sachiaar — aligned with Truth?
↓

ਹੁਕਮਿ ਰਜਾਈ ਚਲਣਾ

Live in harmony with Hukam.

⸻

Why Pauri 2 Naturally Follows

Guru Nanak has just given us the answer:

Live according to Hukam.

That immediately creates another question:

What exactly is Hukam?

And that is precisely where Pauri 2 begins:

ਹੁਕਮੀ ਹੋਵਨਿ ਆਕਾਰ ਹੁਕਮੁ ਨ ਕਹਿਆ ਜਾਈ ॥

Hukmi hovan aakaar, hukam na kahia jaai.

Through Hukam, forms come into existence — yet Hukam itself cannot be completely described.

So Pauri 1 asks how to live in Truth; Pauri 2 begins explaining the nature of the Hukam through which all Reality unfolds.
